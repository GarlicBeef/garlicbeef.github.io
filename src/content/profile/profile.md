I'm an experimental physicist at the Max Planck Institute of Quantum Optics in Garching. I joined Immanuel Bloch's group as a postdoc in 2024 and have been a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Fellow there since 2025. I work with ultracold sodium-potassium molecules, studying how strong dipolar interactions shape many-body physics. Part of that work is building the high-power microwave system that keeps the molecules stable. Recently our team observed a controlled deformation of the Fermi surface in a gas of fermionic molecules, the first many-body effect seen in this system.

I did my PhD at the Centre for Quantum Technologies in Singapore with Huanqian Loh, where I built a rubidium atom-array platform from scratch, with 400 optical tweezers in a triangular lattice. I developed a parallel rearrangement algorithm that sorts atoms into defect-free arrays of up to 225 atoms, about four times faster than earlier multi-tweezer methods. It was published in Physical Review Applied and featured in Physics. On the same platform we added Rydberg excitation and observed quantum thermalization restricted to Hilbert space fragments. Before that, I studied physics at Zhejiang University, building precision current controllers and laser locks in Bo Yan's lab.

I like building experiments from the ground up: vacuum systems, lasers, optics, microwaves and the code that ties them together. Outside the lab I watch a lot of films, and lately I've taken up diving. The Field Notes below collect some of both.
